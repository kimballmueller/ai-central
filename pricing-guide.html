01 · Where It Breaks

A website can describe a business, or it can run part of one.

Most of them do the first. That was the right build ten years ago, when the job was to look legitimate and list a phone number. It is not the job anymore. The site is now the first and often the only conversation a buyer has with you before they decide, and in most businesses it is the only part of the operation nobody has looked at in three years.

🕰️

It waits

Someone finds the site, reads it, and leaves. No name captured, nothing follows. The visit is spent and you never knew it happened.

🎯

It answers the wrong question

Built to explain who the company is. Not built for the specific thing the buyer typed at nine on a Sunday night, which is what actually decided it.

📥

It ends at the form

A submission lands in an inbox. Everything after that point depends on somebody remembering. On a busy week, nobody does.

Traffic is rarely the real problem. The problem is that nothing happens to the traffic once it arrives.

This is the gap the pricing below is built around.

02 · How People Find You Now

One question. Three engines answering it.

Two years ago "getting found" meant one thing: ranking on Google. It has quietly split into three, and they reward different work. A business doing only the first is getting a shrinking share of a growing number of searches.

SEO

Search Engine Optimization

Ranking on Google when somebody types the thing you do. Still the largest single source of buying intent, still what everyone means when they say SEO. Service pages, site speed, local profiles, the map pack, the technical work underneath all of it.

AEO

Answer Engine Optimization

Getting your content quoted in the answer instead of listed under it. AI Overviews, featured snippets, voice results. It runs on structure: schema markup, clean headings, and pages written as the questions people actually ask.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimization

Getting your brand named when somebody asks ChatGPT or Gemini "who should I use for this?" There are no blue links in that answer. There are two or three names, and whoever gets named first gets the call.

The Princeton GEO study measured what actually makes a generative engine surface a source, across roughly 10,000 queries. Three levers moved visibility in AI answers by roughly 30 to 40 percent each:

Quotations

Direct quotes from named people

Statistics

Concrete numbers, cited

Citations

Referenced, attributable sources

Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024

These are not three vendors and three invoices. They are one workstream. The same page, structured correctly, ranks on Google, gets quoted in the answer box, and gives the model something to cite when it recommends someone. Split across three specialists, it costs three times as much and the work contradicts itself.
