Before we go further

This is a relationship business. The marketing has to respect that.

"We embed ourselves in the company. Our goal is to make ourselves invaluable. We build deep, deep, long-standing relationships."

— Richard, describing how Blalock works with clients

Nobody hands over their books, their receivables, and eventually their company because a well-targeted ad found them. They do it because someone they trust vouched for you, or because the first thing you said demonstrated you already understood their situation. That is how this industry has always worked, and no amount of technology changes it.

So it's worth being clear about what this engagement is not. It is not ten thousand Meta ads and a dashboard. We will test a great deal — messaging, channels, partner categories, industries — because you have to test to find what works. But the thing being tested is always quality: did this produce a conversation with someone who genuinely fits, and did we earn the right to have it?

Everything after this section is built around that. The trigger data in section 05 exists so the first sentence out of your mouth is informed rather than generic. The partner program in section 07 exists because a warm introduction outperforms anything we could buy. The content in section 06 exists so that when someone checks you out, they find a person with a track record instead of a logo. And the scoreboard in section 12 counts conversations and qualified opportunities — never impressions, never clicks, never emails sent.

Put simply

The way you serve clients is the way we intend to go and find them. Slowly enough to be credible, systematically enough to be repeatable, and at a volume small enough that every single touch can still be worth receiving.

13 — Questions You're Going To Ask

The honest answers.

How fast will we actually see leads?

A meeting out of the broker channel and your two live deals inside 30 days. A qualified opportunity from partners or triggered outbound by day 45–75. A closed client somewhere in month three to five. That last number is set by your sales cycle rather than our speed — you told me a big fish takes two or three months to digest, and the decision to hand over your books and your capital structure isn't quick either.

Anyone promising closed business in thirty days in this category is either selling merchant cash advances or lying to you.

What if the trigger data doesn't produce?

Then the partner channel carries the year, which is exactly why we build it first and why it's the channel we're most confident in. These are deliberately independent bets — public-record data and human referral networks fail for completely different reasons, so they're unlikely to fail together. We review every channel at day 90 and move budget to whatever is producing qualified opportunities. Nothing in this plan is precious.

We're hiring someone with an IT and accounting background. Why not just have her do this?

She should — eventually, and we build so that she can. But she is one person starting a new job, and this is a website rebuild, a CRM implementation, a data pipeline across four public sources, warmed email infrastructure, a partner recruitment program, and a content operation. That's a team's first six months, not one new hire's.

The realistic version is that we build it, she learns it alongside us, and somewhere in year two she owns most of it. That's a better outcome for Blalock than either of us doing it alone — and it's precisely why nothing gets built in our accounts.

Who owns everything if we part ways?

You do, entirely, including the parts that are expensive to rebuild. Every account is registered in Blalock's name on day one — the website, the CRM and all its data, the domains, the sequences, the signal pipeline, and the partner list with its commission history. There is no version of this where leaving costs you your infrastructure.

For a firm whose entire thesis is ownership and equity, renting your growth infrastructure from a vendor would be a strange way to live.

Why pay for content if it takes a year to work?

Because most of it isn't waiting on search. Case studies and the comparison sheet move your close rate now — and at forty conversations a year, going from 50% to 60% is worth about four extra clients. The Cash Flow Index doesn't depend on domain authority at all, because bankers forward it. Your LinkedIn presence raises the reply rate on outbound that is already running.

The genuinely slow piece is organic search, which we've deliberately scoped at roughly 20% of the effort rather than the centerpiece, and told you plainly is a year-two asset.

Can we start with Foundation and move up later?

You can, and it's month-to-month either way. But it's worth being honest about what $3,500 buys: the infrastructure gets built, and one channel runs. The signal pipeline — the part that makes 750 prospects outperform 40,000 — isn't in it, and neither is the partner recruitment engine, which is where we expect your first win to come from.

You'd be paying for the foundation of the house and then waiting a quarter to decide whether you want the house. Given that every month of waiting costs roughly $200,000 in unearned net income by your own arithmetic, the slower option is the expensive one.
