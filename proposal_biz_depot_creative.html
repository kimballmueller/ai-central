Finding 1
Nobody runs real sellers

Across ~120 live ads: brokers talking to camera, stock footage, valuation calculators. Not one ad built around an actual seller who closed. The best creative in this category hasn't been made yet.

Finding 2
The loudest message is anti-broker

"Skip the broker." "2–5x more than a broker." "No brokers, no noise." The category's biggest objection is broker distrust. A broker saying "trust me" can't answer that. A seller who got paid can.

Finding 3
The hooks are knowable

The ads that survive months of spend open with second-person pain, money math, or a direct question — and close with a free valuation plus a confidentiality promise. We don't have to guess what works. It's public.

The takeaway: your competitors' ads all say "trust me." You're about to have 12 closed sellers who can say "I did, and here's my number." That's creative no competitor can copy without your deal flow — and it answers the exact objection the whole category is fighting.

02 · The Creative

Each Shoot Is Engineered to Perform, Not Just Look Good

A testimonial that only lives on your website is a trophy. These are built as ad creative from the first minute of the interview. Three mechanics make that true:

1 · The hook bank First 3 seconds

Viewers decide in under 2 seconds; a 30% three-second view rate is the paid-ads benchmark. So I don't hope a hook shows up — I interview for it. Every seller session is run to capture 3–5 openable moments in their own words, each matching a hook type already proven in this auction: the pain hook, the money-math hook, the direct question. If the seller says something better than the plan, that becomes the ad. It usually does.

For scale of what "proven" means: SMB Deal Hunter is currently paying to run "25 years in. You're tired. Your body knows it." — that's the emotional register the auction rewards. Ours comes from a real person who actually lived it.
2 · The cutdown matrix One shoot → 10+ ads

Every long-running advertiser in the table above runs multiple versions of the same creative — iteration is how ads win. I structure the interview so every answer stands alone (the seller folds the question into the answer). A 60–90 minute session cuts into one flagship film plus 8–12 vertical cutdowns: 3–5 hooks × 2–3 bodies. Your media buyer gets a testable matrix per seller, not a single video to pray over.

And because the 12 sellers span landscaping, dumpsters, electrical, automotive, glass, and more, you get a vertical-matched ad for each trade — the same targeting play Wisable is running right now with its auto-shop-only creative, except with a real owner from that trade on screen.

3 · Documentary real, not scripted Authenticity converts

The data is one-directional: authentic, UGC-style ads pull roughly 4x the click-through at about half the cost-per-click of polished brand ads, testimonials lift conversions by up to a third, and over-scripted "ring light" testimonials are actively dying in the auction. So no seller ever gets handed a line. I research them for a week, we talk before I fly out, and on the day it's a conversation shot like a documentary — cinematic lighting, real location, real speech patterns. It looks premium and reads true, and those aren't in tension when it's shot right.

The frame, exactly as I build it

Two cameras, one operator. The wide is composed so the same take reframes cleanly to 9:16 — every interview is shot for the feed, not cropped for it as an afterthought.

A-cam · 16:9 wide Subject on the right third · shop visible behind, lit for depth
B-cam · 16:9 tight Covers every edit point · makes tight cutdowns invisible
9:16 cutdown · framed in-camera

The A-cam wide keeps the seller inside the vertical crop line, so cutdowns don't lose the frame. Hook text sits top-center where the eye lands, captions burn in above the platform UI (85% of feed video plays muted), and the right rail stays clear of Reels buttons. Every cutdown ships captioned, color-graded, and platform-safe.

03 · The Script

The Testimonial Arc — Their Story, Structured to Prove BizDepot

No seller reads lines. The "script" is a question plan I build per seller — a week of research on them and their business, facts verified, a group text with them and your team before I fly out. Then it gets asked like a conversation, because that's what it is.

But an unstructured story produces a nice video and no proof. So every interview runs the same six beats. Four of them carry the seller's story — that's what makes it watchable. Two of them are where BizDepot gets proven, and those two get the most screen time in the finished cut.

Questions get customized per seller — a 30-year landscaping company and a 4-year dumpster business don't get the same interview. The arc doesn't change.

Beat 01The Owner
Beat 02The Hesitation
Beat 03Why BizDepot
Beat 04How It Actually Went
Beat 05The Outcome
Beat 06To the Next Owner

The two filled beats are the credibility core — roughly half the running time of the flagship film.

1
The Owner Proves: a real peer, not an actor
"Tell me what you built — how long, how it started, how big it got."
"What did a normal Tuesday look like for you five years ago?"

An owner in that same trade recognizes himself in the first five seconds. That recognition is what buys the next twenty-five.

2
The Hesitation Proves: the objection is understood
"How long were you thinking about selling before you actually did anything about it?"
"What were you afraid would happen if you tried?"
"Be straight with me — what did you think of business brokers before this?"

This beat is aimed directly at Finding 2. Skepticism that gets voiced and then resolved by the person who lived it is the one message the "skip the broker" ads can't answer.

3
Why BizDepot Proves: straight talk, no surprises
"What made you finally pick up the phone — and why BizDepot?"
"What was that first conversation like? What did they tell you the business was worth, and how did they get to that number?"
"Did anything they said surprise you — good or bad?"
"Did the deal land where they told you it would?"

The valuation conversation is where trust is won or lost in this category. I want the seller describing how BizDepot arrived at the number and whether the close matched the promise — proven in their words, not asserted in a voiceover.

4
How It Actually Went Proves: hands-off, and the buyers were real
"Walk me through it start to finish — first call to closing day. How long did it take?"
"How much of this did you actually have to do? What did BizDepot handle that you assumed would land on you?"
"Were you still running the business the whole time?"
"Tell me about the buyers — how many did you meet, and were they serious?"
"Was there a point where you thought the deal was dead? What happened?"

The heaviest beat in the cut, and specifics only. "They were great" never makes it; "I signed a handful of documents, met two real buyers, and never missed a day of work" does. The dead-deal question earns its place — a process with no friction sounds fake, and friction that BizDepot cleared is the strongest proof in the film.

5
The Outcome Proves: the payoff is real and numeric
"What did you think it was worth before anyone looked at it? What did it actually sell for?"
"What did selling actually change for you and your family?"

Named numbers, named timelines. This is the beat that feeds the money-math hook the auction rewards — and the statistics-and-quotations levers the GEO research points to in Section 05.

6
To the Next Owner Proves: the CTA comes from a peer
"Somebody's watching this who's been thinking about selling for two years and hasn't called anyone. Talk to them."
"What would you say to an owner who figures nobody can sell their business but them?"

The call to action comes from the seller, not a logo card. When the buyer's on site, I interview them separately and add the handoff — which also gives you buyer-facing creative from the same day.

The arc is also the assembly line

Beats 1–2 produce the hooks. Beats 3–4 produce the bodies — the ease-and-credibility payload that makes the ad worth watching past three seconds. Beats 5–6 produce the closes. That's why one 60–90 minute session yields 8–12 testable cutdowns instead of a single video: the structure that makes the testimonial credible is the same structure that makes the matrix.

On going off-script: I've run about 100 of these interviews. The plan above gets the footage the ads need; the best moment of the day is usually the one no plan saw coming. I follow it when it appears, then bring the conversation back. That instinct is the difference between coverage and creative.

04 · AI Search, Part 1

AEO — Built to Be the Answer

Answer Engine Optimization: when an owner asks Google's AI Overviews or Perplexity "how do I sell my landscaping business," your content is the answer that gets pulled. Video is eligible for that — but engines read video as text: the transcript, the chapters, and the page it's embedded on. Three facts shape the plan:

30x

Google AI Overviews cites YouTube ~30x more often than ChatGPT does — YouTube + Google surfaces are where video AEO pays.

75%

Perplexity and AI Overviews together drive ~75% of YouTube citations in AI answers.

≈0

Correlation between view count and AI citation. A 200-view video that answers the question directly can beat a 500K-subscriber channel.

That last number is the opportunity: BizDepot doesn't need a big channel to win AI search. It needs videos that directly answer the questions owners actually ask. Which is exactly what a well-run seller interview is — so I structure it that way on purpose:

Question-framed segments. Interview sections map to real queries: "How much is a landscaping business worth?" "Can I sell without my employees finding out?" "Do I need a broker?" Each answer is a citable unit.

Full transcript with every video — the single highest-impact AEO asset, because the transcript is the text engines actually quote.

Chapters and timestamps named as questions, so engines can cite the exact segment.

A page-ready embed kit: VideoObject + FAQ schema and an FAQ text block for each video's page on bizdepot's site, connecting the video to crawlable text. Your web team pastes it in; done.

05 · AI Search, Part 2

GEO — Built to Be the Brand AI Recommends

Generative Engine Optimization is the other half, and it's different: AEO gets your content quoted; GEO gets your brand named when someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini "should I use a business broker?" or "who sells plumbing businesses in the Carolinas?" By 2030, a large share of seller journeys starts with that question asked to an AI. Whoever the models name first gets the call.

The Princeton GEO study (KDD 2024, ~10,000 queries) measured what makes generative engines surface a source. The three biggest levers — each worth roughly 30–40% more visibility in AI answers:

Quotations

Direct quotes from named people

Statistics

Concrete numbers, not adjectives

Cited sources

Third-party corroboration

Look at what a seller-testimonial library produces natively: real quotations from named owners. Real deal statistics — timelines, industries, outcomes. And third-party corroboration, because it's the seller's own words on the record, not BizDepot talking about itself. The exact three things the research says AI engines reward, generated as a byproduct of filming.

The entity play: 12 named sellers, in 12 named trades, in named cities — landscaping in Amarillo, dumpsters in Des Moines, electrical in upstate New York — every one co-occurring with "BizDepot" across YouTube, transcripts, and site pages. That's what teaches retrieval systems that BizDepot is the answer to "business broker for [trade] in [region]." Nobody wins that with one video. You win it with a library — and you're the only brokerage building one.

The flywheel

A deal closes → I film the seller within weeks, while the story is fresh.

One shoot becomes 10+ assets → flagship for listing calls and the website, cutdown matrix for paid, transcript + schema kit for AI search.

Ads run vertical-matched → the roofing owner sees a roofing seller who got paid, not a broker's pitch.

Competitors can't follow → they'd need your closed deals to make this creative. Every video widens the gap.

And we measure it like media, not like art

Hook rate against the 30% three-second benchmark. CPL by vertical. Which hooks and which sellers drive booked valuation calls. I review the numbers with your team after each batch, and the next round of interviews leans into what's converting. Twelve videos isn't the deliverable — it's twelve tests that make every following video cheaper and sharper.
