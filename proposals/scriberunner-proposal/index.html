Where the money actually shows up

Three months, three different kinds of revenue. Only one of them arrives immediately, and we would rather tell you that now than in week six.

MONTH ONE

Revenue from the book you already have

The cross-sell map lands in week two. These are practices that already trust you and already pay you — no warm-up, no new relationship, no waiting on an inbox to establish. It is the only revenue in this plan that does not have to wait for anything.

MONTH TWO

Demos, not yet signatures

Sending addresses have finished establishing and outreach goes live. Your calendar starts filling with practices you have never spoken to. Expect pipeline this month, not revenue — and be suspicious of anyone who promises you otherwise.

MONTH THREE

Cold outreach becomes clients

The demos booked in month two close, the January follow-ups come back, and the month-one clients get looked at for a second service line. By the end of it you know your cost per client — which is what makes month four a decision instead of a hope.

What keeps it working

Three things drift on their own: what lands in an inbox, who is worth reaching, and what the system believes a reply means. Left alone, outreach that worked in month two is quietly worse by month six — not broken, just less right. So there is a standing review, weekly at first and monthly once the numbers settle.

  1. Read what actually went outEvery review

    Not a sample of templates — the real emails, sent to real practices, and the replies they got back. Copy that has gone stale gets rewritten, and anything that reads like a robot wrote it gets cut before your name is attached to it twice.

  2. Check who is being reachedEvery review

    The accounts that scored well and the ones that didn't, side by side against the clients you actually signed. Your definition of a good practice will move as you learn which ones close — the scoring has to move with it, or you spend month six chasing month one's idea of a good fit.

  3. Audit what the system decidedEvery review

    Spot-check the replies it classified. If it called something interested that wasn't, or filed a real opportunity as a polite no, that gets corrected and the rule behind it changes. This is the check that protects your calendar — a system that misreads replies wastes your time before it wastes anything else.

  4. Cut, and double downMonthly

    Specialties, service lines and messages that are producing get more volume. The ones that are not get stopped. Every month the system should be pointed slightly more precisely than it was the month before.

What this does not do

We are not going to tell you that an outreach system closes anyone. You close. Every demo on that calendar is still yours to run, and revenue cycle management — the line you said is hardest to win because nobody hands over their billing to a stranger — will stay a relationship sale for as long as you are in business. Nothing here shortens that trust. What changes is which conversations reach you, and what condition they arrive in.

On the agents you tried yourself, the ones that ran slowly and burned through your tokens without impressing you: that is the normal result of asking a chatbot to do a machine's job. Most of what you described does not need a model at all. Checking whether a practice is already in your CRM is a database question, not a reasoning one, and answered that way it costs a fraction of a cent instead of a minute of thinking. The expensive reasoning gets spent in the two places it earns its keep — deciding which of your services to lead with, and writing the email that opens the conversation.

Two numbers in this document are yours rather than ours, and they should be settled at kickoff rather than assumed here: what your margin actually is, and what a typical account is worth once it is buying more than one service. Both move the arithmetic in section 04 considerably, and both are knowable in an afternoon with your own books open.

And one thing worth saying plainly before you sign anything: nothing in here touches patient information. This system works with public practice data, business contacts, your CRM and your outbound. It is never connected to the SAY app, to any EHR, or to anything carrying clinical records — not in month three and not later. That boundary is a design decision, not a limitation, and it is what keeps this out of your compliance team's way entirely.
