02

What's in the way

Three constraints, in the order they cost you the most.

01

Every deal has to pass through you.

Five leads a day is already more than one person can work properly, and you are also the one servicing the existing book. So leads sit. In this business a lead that sits for a week is not a slower lead, it is a dead one — the owner either signed with whoever called back first or forgot the problem entirely. This is the expensive one, because it caps every other thing on this page.

02

The hundred merchants you already have are not being worked.

The relationship currently ends at the statement. But merchant services is a referral business — an owner who trusts you with their deposits knows six other owners in the same plaza with the same processor and the same complaints. Right now nothing systematically asks them, and nothing notices when one of them starts processing less.

03

Cold dialing is the only way in, and it strips out your advantage.

The dialer produces volume, but it puts a rep in front of a stranger with no reason to believe anything. Your edge — fewer holds, faster approval, underwriting that understands cash-heavy businesses — is a proof argument. Proof needs a warm audience and something to point at. A cold call gives it neither.

What this does not do

We are not going to tell you that a marketing program manufactures merchants. Nothing here dials faster than your dialer or writes an approval you would not have written yourself. Closing is still you, and for a while it has to be. What changes is which conversations reach you, and in what condition they arrive.

On buying an off-the-shelf AI assistant: they are real, they work, and a few hundred a month is a fair price for what they do. But every one of them is a front-of-funnel tool, and you do not have a front-of-funnel problem. You told us you get five leads a day and close few of them. Pouring more volume into a single closer makes the queue longer, not the month better. Buy one if you want the nurture — just do not expect it to move the number in section 01, because the number in section 01 is capped by your calendar.

On putting your name on someone else's platform and reselling it: we would think hard about that one. Your residual book is the durable asset here — a merchant relationship that pays every month for years and gets more valuable the longer it runs. A resold software subscription is month to month for you, month to month for them, and built on a roadmap you do not control. If it breaks or the vendor changes direction, it breaks in front of the merchant whose processing you also hold. That trades an annuity you own for a margin you rent, and it puts the annuity at risk to do it.

There is also a version of the reseller pitch worth checking before you sign anything: confirm in writing that a white-label or reseller tier actually exists, what it costs, who owns the merchant relationship, and who the customer belongs to if you stop. Some of these programs are affiliate arrangements rather than reseller ones, which is a different deal than it sounds like on a call.

Before you decide

Four questions worth answering in writing rather than on a call.

Q1

Do I still need to hire sales reps?

Eventually, yes, and you should. Nothing here replaces a closer. What it does is make the hire work — a commission rep who joins a business with no pipeline, no follow-up and no proof library quits in six weeks and tells people why. The same rep joining a business where qualified appointments already arrive on a schedule has something to close on day one.

The order matters more than the timing. Build the pipeline, then hire into it.

Q2

What happens to the dialer?

It keeps running. We are not asking you to switch off something that produces five leads a day. What changes is where those leads land: today they land on you, and the ones you cannot get to that afternoon quietly expire. Routed into the CRM with automatic follow-up, the same five leads a day produce more without anyone dialing more.

Q3

Does any of this touch my merchant relationships?

Only in the direction of strengthening them. We do not sell anything to your book, we do not put a third-party product in front of them, and we do not contact them about anything except your own service and referrals. Your processing relationship is the asset. Nothing we build gets in front of it.

Q4

What do you report on?

Not impressions and not followers. Four numbers, monthly: merchants contacted from the book, referrals generated, qualified appointments set, and merchants boarded. The last one is the only one that pays you, and the other three exist to explain it.

We will also report the number that is easy to hide from — leads that went untouched — because that figure is the entire reason this proposal exists, and it should go down every month.
