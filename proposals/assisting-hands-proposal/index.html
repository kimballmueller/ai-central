02

What's in the way

Three constraints, in the order they cost you the most. All three are mechanical, and you can verify every number in this section yourself.

01

Nine months of good writing is invisible to the machines that now do the recommending.

Across your last twenty posts there are eleven heading tags in total — roughly one every two articles. Your writer is already writing subheadings; they are just not marked as subheadings. In your September 22nd post, "The Numbers Tell a Real Story" is a paragraph tag. To a human reader the page looks fine. To a crawler or a language model it is an undifferentiated wall of text with nothing to lift out and quote. The work is done. The formatting is throwing it away.

02

Your site tells Google what it is, but never tells it what you are.

Your location page carries WebPage and Breadcrumb markup and nothing else. There is no LocalBusiness record, no FAQ markup, and no review markup anywhere on the site. Your H1 is "Quality Home Care You Can Trust" — a brand promise with no service and no city in it. Your sister agency in Naperville, on the same corporate WordPress, leads with city, county and phone number.

03

Every lead you pay for, four competitors are also paying for.

A Place for Mom sells the same enquiry to up to five agencies. You have built real discipline around mining that list, and it works — you have closed people in June who first called in April. But it is a channel where you compete on response speed forever and never accumulate an advantage. The referral route you actually want, the five or six facilities, has no digital component at all right now.

Site audited 30 September 2026 against assistinghands.com/127/illinois/chicagonorth and its public WordPress API. One correction worth having: you said you publish two blogs a month. You are actually publishing weekly — 37 posts, every Tuesday, for nine months straight. That matters, because it means cadence was never your problem, which is exactly why every agency that told you to write more blogs was never going to help you.

04

Who we're reaching

Two buyers who could not be less alike. One arrives at midnight through a search box. The other has to be walked in to, in person, and will never click an ad.

Audience one

The adult child, usually the daughter

Forty-five to sixty-five, lives in or near your territory, and is researching at eleven at night after a bad phone call with a parent. She does not search "home care." She searches the situation — her father fell, her mother is not eating, the hospital is discharging on Thursday and nobody is ready. She asks in plain sentences, increasingly to an AI rather than to Google.

Reached through structured answersBecause she asks in questions, the page that wins is the one shaped as a question with the answer directly beneath it. That is a formatting decision, not a writing one, and it is the single cheapest thing on this list to fix.
Audience two

The discharge planner and case manager

Inside the five or six facilities you have targeted. Overworked, loyal to whoever they already use, and protected by a gatekeeper. There is no digital channel that reaches this person directly, and we will not pretend otherwise. They are not on LinkedIn, you have already tested that, and six accounts is far too small a universe for paid media.

Reached in person, backed by proofYou walking in is the channel. What we build is what they find when they look you up afterwards, which is the part that decides whether the second visit goes differently from the first.

Three doors into the facilities

None of these replace your rounds. Each one makes a round land harder, and each is a system we build and run.

DOOR 01

A page per facility

One private landing page per target, reached by a QR code on whatever you hand them. Named for them, carrying testimonials from families they would recognise. You find out who looked, and when.

DOOR 02

The television segment, cut up

You paid real money for that authority and it is currently sitting in one place. As-seen-on-TV is the most credible asset you own with this audience, and it should be on the page they land on, in the email you send, and behind you when you walk in.

DOOR 03

Open-tracking you already have

Mailchimp is already telling you which case managers open your emails. Right now that data dies in a report. Wired into the system, it tells you who to visit this week rather than who is next in the rotation.
