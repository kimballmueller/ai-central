05 — The Fastest Fix You Have

You move hundreds of people a month and collect almost no proof of it.

Every other item in this proposal takes weeks to compound. This one doesn't. Right now a bus leaves with forty passengers on it, delivers a flawless day, and generates zero public evidence that it happened. The cricket team was thrilled — your words: "if I'd have had a QR code or something for them to leave a Google review, we would have probably got raving reviews. But we don't have that."

Get the profile verified, first

The shared address is a solvable problem — it's a common one, and there are documented routes through it. Until that video verification clears, you are invisible in the map pack and largely invisible to the AI tools, which read Google before almost anything else.

Ask everyone on the bus, not just the booker

You already have the manifest. One completed trip becomes twenty review requests instead of one — automated, sent the same day while the experience is fresh, and routed so five-star ratings go to Google and anything lower comes back to you as private feedback first.

Recency is what actually ranks now

Google has been explicit that fresh, relevant content and recent signals outrank age. Ten years in business doesn't beat a competitor collecting reviews every week. Run this for six months and you pass operators who've been in Dallas twice as long.

This is also the thing costing you contracts you've already half-won.

You described the university that made you a preferred vendor and then put more work out for quote — "part of their process, I'm sure, is going to go look at reviews." Same for the DMCs, same for corporate travel managers, same for every parent researching a school charter. A verified profile with steady five-star volume isn't a marketing nicety in a business where you're putting strangers' children and executives on a vehicle. It's the trust check that happens right before the signature.
