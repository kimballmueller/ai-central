Your checklist

Six things to have ready

Tap each one as you knock it out — this page remembers.

Block about four hours

Protect the window on your calendar. The interview itself is 60–90 minutes; the rest is setup and filming your business.

Pick one quiet indoor room

Where we sit down for the interview. Let your team, family and front desk know that room is off-limits for about 90 minutes.

  • Reasonably spacious
  • Nobody walking through
  • Two power outlets
  • Phones on silent
  • HVAC, TV, radio, compressors we can pause if they're loud
  • A backup room, in case the first one turns out noisy

Have the business looking sharp

This is where the film gets its look. If we're featuring trucks or major equipment, have them on site and reasonably clean.

  • Exterior and signage
  • The yard or property
  • Trucks and trailers
  • Equipment, mowers, tools
  • Your office and desk
  • Anything you're proud of

Keep your people around

Don't clear the place out. A working business films far better than an empty one — crews doing normal work, people in the office, the phone ringing.

The only thing we protect is the interview room while we're recording.

Dig out the old photosMost important

This is the one that makes the film feel like a story instead of a talking head. Pile everything in one place before I arrive. Blurry old phone photos absolutely count.

  • The first truck
  • The first shop or office
  • Old company photos
  • Old business cards or logo
  • Original uniforms or shirts
  • Newspaper clippings, awards
  • Early job or customer photos
  • You, back when you started
  • Family in the business
  • Anything from the early years

Bring two shirts

One you'd normally wear to work — company polo, branded button-down. And one a step nicer. We'll pick on the day. You should look like yourself, not like you're going on CNBC.

Works great

Solid colors · mid-tones · simple textures

Causes problems on camera

Tiny stripes · small checks · herringbone · shiny or reflective fabric · big unrelated logos · noisy jewelry

One more thing

Please don't prepare a speech

Seriously. Don't rehearse, don't write anything down, and I'm not sending you the questions ahead of time — on purpose. Rehearsed answers sound rehearsed, and the real version is always better.

If you want to think about anything, just let a few memories float up beforehand:

  • Starting the company
  • A hard stretch you got through
  • The breakthrough that changed things
  • When you first thought about selling
  • Your first call with BizDepot
  • A stressful moment during the sale
  • Closing day
  • How life feels now
