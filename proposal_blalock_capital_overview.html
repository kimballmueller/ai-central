Four reasons it's happening

None of them are market problems. The market is enormous, and you're one of very few firms serving it whole.

01

Marketing stops the second a deal closes

A big client lands, it takes two or three months to absorb, and the top of the funnel goes dark for a quarter. Then it restarts from zero.

"We get a big fish on the hook, it'll take two or three months to digest that client. And so we're not doing any marketing during that time."
02

There's nowhere for a slow reply to land

You emailed a few hundred contacts and heard almost nothing. You called that same list and booked appointments. The list was never scored, the message was never tested, and nothing tracked the follow-up.

"Hundreds and hundreds of emails gone in, hardly any answers at all. But I would take those lists and I would call, and I would get appointments."
03

One channel, and it's thinning

The equipment broker channel genuinely works. But out of fifty customers a broker might have three that fit. It's a good channel. It can't be the only one.

"I've got four or five meetings through them, but I think their pipeline's drying up."
04

The website can't take a handoff

Clicking "working capital financing" currently lands on an AI readiness page. Every bit of interest anyone creates leaks out right there.

"That's our number one thing we've got going on right now."
In one sentence

You close half the deals that reach you. Not enough of them reach you.

A referred deal closes at roughly 50%. A client is worth about $500k to you across advisory, financing, and the eventual exit. Your financing partner has capacity for four or five more clients today, with a private equity partner ready behind her. Every constraint on growth has already been solved except the one that finds people to sell to.
