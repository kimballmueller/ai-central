The role

What the role actually owns.

  • Well planning through execution, and the AFE discipline that decides whether the programme lands on budget.
  • Vendor and service company management, where most of the recoverable cost sits.
  • Field presence — how much, and whether the candidate genuinely wants it.
  • Completions design and the offset analysis behind it, in an unconventional programme.

Our screen

How we screen for it.

01

Segment and asset

Upstream, midstream or downstream — and which basin, which formation, which plant. Permian unconventional and deepwater Gulf are different jobs with the same job title.

02

Cycle behaviour

What they did in 2015 and in 2020. How a candidate behaved in a downturn predicts the next five years better than the résumé does.

03

Operator or service side

Whether they have sat on the operator's side of the table or the vendor's. It changes how they manage cost.

04

Budget dependence

Whether somebody who thrived at a supermajor can operate without that budget behind them.

FAQ

Common questions.

What does a drilling engineer earn?

Currently roughly $145,000 to $185,000 depending on basin, operator size and rotation. Production superintendents, turnaround managers and project controls leads sit in adjacent bands.

Do you recruit outside the Gulf Coast?

Yes, nationwide. Our deepest technical network is on the Gulf Coast — including the hard‑to‑fill subsea and ROV roles where the candidate pool is a set of relationships rather than a job board — but the drilling and completions work follows the basins.

How do your fees work?

A percentage of first‑year compensation, structured to the level of the role and the volume of work, and agreed in writing before the search begins.

How long does a search usually take?

For most roles the first slate reaches you in under 10 days from the briefing. We then take your feedback and come back with a tighter four. Time to offer depends more on your interview process than on ours.
