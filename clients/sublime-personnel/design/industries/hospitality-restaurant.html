Hospitality & Restaurant Recruiting

There is a difference between a résumé that reads like a general manager and a person who can hold a building together on a Saturday night. Thirty years of restaurant operations sit behind every search we run in this practice.

Why us for this

We ran these
buildings first.

Pete Proctor spent more than thirty years in restaurant operations before he recruited for it — walking into units in the low nineties and taking them past one‑thirty. That background is the screen. When a candidate describes how they fixed a labour line or turned a kitchen culture around, we know within a few minutes whether the story is real.

Hospitality hiring fails in predictable places: a strong operator who has never run more than one unit, a chef who can cook but cannot cost, a district manager who has only ever inherited healthy stores. We ask the questions that expose those gaps before you spend three months discovering them.

What we screen for

What separates a résumé from a hire.

01

Unit economics, not anecdotes

We ask for the numbers they owned — food cost, labour, COGS, AUV, flow‑through — and how they moved them. Operators who genuinely ran the P&L answer in seconds.

02

Span of control

A five‑unit district and a twenty‑unit district are different jobs. We map what a candidate actually supervised, including whether they built the bench beneath them.

03

Turnover and retention

Anyone can open a store. We look at whether their teams stayed, and what they did in the first ninety days when they inherited a broken one.

04

Hours and expectations

Hospitality burns people who did not know what they were signing up for. We are direct about schedule, travel and expectations before the first interview.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do you recruit for independents or only large groups?

Both. Our client profile is a company that can support roughly four searches a year — usually a multi‑unit group, a growing regional brand or a hospitality management company — but we take strong independent operators when the role is senior enough to justify the work.

Can you fill hourly or line‑level roles?

Our work is management and above. For hourly volume hiring a staffing agency will serve you better, and we will say so rather than take the engagement.

How do your fees work?

A percentage of first‑year compensation, structured to the level of the role and the volume of work, and agreed in writing before the search begins.

How long does a search usually take?

For most roles we present a first slate within two weeks of the briefing. Time to offer depends more on your interview process than on ours.
